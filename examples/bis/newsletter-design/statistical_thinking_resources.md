# Statistical Thinking in Practice: Recommended Online Resources

For the **Royal Statistical Society's Business and Industrial Section (RSS BIS)**, I would suggest building a collection of online resources that demonstrate how statistical methods and statistical thinking contribute to decision-making in industry, business, government and the wider public sector.

I would deliberately go beyond conventional statistics blogs. Some of the most interesting material comes from organisations that use statistics to solve real operational problems, even when they do not explicitly describe their work as *statistics*.

I would organise the collection around six themes:

1. **Statistical thinking and decision-making** — uncertainty, evidence, causality, risk and judgement.
2. **Industrial statistics and quality engineering** — process control, reliability, design of experiments and continuous improvement.
3. **Business analytics and experimentation** — A/B testing, forecasting, customer behaviour and operational decisions.
4. **Public-sector statistics and policy evaluation** — official statistics, evidence-based policy and public communication.
5. **Modern statistical methodology** — Bayesian methods, causal inference, statistical learning and uncertainty quantification.
6. **Statistics in emerging applications** — energy systems, AI, digital twins, climate, healthcare and infrastructure.

An especially useful distinction is between resources that **explain statistical methodology** and those that **document its application to real problems**. A good collection should include both.

## 1. Statistical thinking, communication and decision-making

These resources are particularly suitable for a newsletter aimed at statisticians and people who use statistical evidence in management.

| Resource | Focus | Why it is interesting |
|---|---|---|
| [Statistical Modeling, Causal Inference, and Social Science](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/) | Andrew Gelman's blog | Statistical reasoning, modelling failures, Bayesian inference, reproducibility and criticism of published research. |
| [Significance Magazine](https://academic.oup.com/jrssig) | Applied statistics | Accessible articles showing how statistics contributes to society, commerce, science and public policy. |
| [Understanding Uncertainty](https://understandinguncertainty.org/) | David Spiegelhalter and colleagues | Risk communication, probability, uncertainty and interpretation of statistical evidence. |
| [The Stats Guy](https://thestatsguy.rbind.io/) | Statistical commentary | Accessible discussions of statistical methods and their applications. |
| [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/) | Global statistical evidence | Excellent examples of presenting complex statistical evidence clearly. |
| [The Pudding](https://pudding.cool/) | Visual storytelling with data | Creative examples of exploratory data analysis and interactive statistical communication. |
| [Real World Data Science](https://realworlddatascience.net/) | RSS data science publication | Practical data science, professional practice and applications. |

**Particularly recommended:** Andrew Gelman's blog.

It regularly explores a fundamental question: *What conclusions can we legitimately draw from the available evidence?*

That question is central to statistical thinking in business and government.

Significance is another natural starting point, particularly because its editorial purpose is to make practical statistics accessible beyond the profession. ([Source](https://academic.oup.com/jrssig/pages/about))

## 2. Industrial statistics, engineering and quality improvement

This category deserves substantial attention because industrial statistics is often underrepresented in contemporary data science discussions.

| Resource | Focus | Why it is interesting |
|---|---|---|
| [NIST Engineering Statistics Handbook](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/) | Engineering statistics | Statistical process control, experimental design, reliability and process improvement. |
| [American Society for Quality](https://asq.org/quality-resources) | Quality engineering | Practical methods for improving industrial processes and measuring performance. |
| [Minitab Blog](https://blog.minitab.com/) | Applied industrial statistics | Case studies involving process capability, control charts, experimental design and quality improvement. |
| [JMP Blog](https://community.jmp.com/t5/JMP-Blog/bg-p/jmp-blog) | Statistical discovery | Experimental design, industrial modelling, visual analytics and statistical applications. |
| [The Deming Institute](https://deming.org/) | Statistical thinking in management | Understanding variation, systems thinking and organisational improvement. |
| [iSixSigma](https://www.isixsigma.com/) | Process improvement | Six Sigma methods, quality control and operational improvement. |

**Particularly recommended:** NIST and the Deming Institute.

NIST's handbook is especially valuable because it approaches statistical methods through engineering problems rather than purely mathematical formulations. Its coverage includes measurement, production processes, reliability and experimental design. ([Source](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/nistsematech-engineering-statistics-handbook))

The Deming Institute provides a complementary perspective: statistical thinking is not simply about applying statistical techniques but about understanding systems and distinguishing meaningful changes from ordinary variation.

Potential newsletter topics include:

- Why managers often mistake random variation for performance improvement.
- Statistical process control outside manufacturing.
- How experimental design can reduce development costs.
- The distinction between process stability and process capability.
- Reliability engineering and predictive maintenance.

This is a particularly rich area because major technology companies increasingly publish articles describing their statistical practices.

| [Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/) | General applied data science | Broad coverage of statistical modelling and business applications; quality varies by author. |

### A particularly interesting example: Microsoft

Microsoft's Experimentation Platform is a strong example of statistics functioning as critical business infrastructure.

Its work encompasses experimental design, trustworthy analysis and the operational challenges of running controlled experiments across major software products. ([Source](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/group/experimentation-platform-exp/))

This provides material for several excellent discussions:

**When should a business trust an A/B test?**

**What happens when experiments interfere with each other?**

**Can statistically significant improvements be commercially irrelevant?**

**How should organisations balance experimentation with operational risk?**

These are statistical questions with immediate business implications.

**Particularly recommended:** ONS Data Science Campus and the Office for Statistics Regulation.

The Office for Statistics Regulation offers a different perspective: what constitutes trustworthy statistical evidence, and how should it be communicated?

A newsletter could usefully feature examples of statistical methodology being applied to public administration, followed by discussions of the limitations of those approaches.

## 5. Modern statistical methodology and causal inference

These resources are more technical but can provide valuable material for readers with a stronger statistical background.

| Resource | Focus | Why it is interesting |
|---|---|---|
| [The Gradient](https://thegradient.pub/) | Machine learning research | Accessible discussions of methodology, limitations and emerging research. |
| [Distill](https://distill.pub/) | Interpretable ML research | Outstanding visual explanations of technical ideas; an archival rather than actively publishing resource. |
| [PyMC Blog](https://www.pymc.io/blog/) | Bayesian modelling | Practical probabilistic programming and Bayesian inference. |
| [Stan](https://mc-stan.org/) | Bayesian computation | Statistical modelling, inference and computational methods. |
| [Causal Inference for the Brave and True](https://matheusfacure.github.io/python-causality-handbook/) | Causal inference | Practical explanations with Python examples and business applications. |
| [Mostly Harmless Econometrics](https://www.mostlyharmlesseconometrics.com/) | Applied econometrics | Causal inference, identification and empirical methodology. |
| [R-bloggers](https://www.r-bloggers.com/) | Statistical computing | Aggregated articles on R, statistics, modelling and data analysis. |
| [Cross Validated](https://stats.stackexchange.com/) | Statistical questions | Real methodological problems and detailed community discussions. |

I would particularly encourage coverage of **causal inference**.

Many organisations are good at measuring correlations but much less successful at establishing whether their interventions actually cause the outcomes they observe.

This applies equally to marketing campaigns, government policies, healthcare interventions and industrial process changes.

## 6. Statistics in energy, infrastructure and complex systems

This is a category I would add specifically because it offers excellent opportunities to connect statistical methods with optimisation, operational research and engineering.

| Resource | Focus | Why it is interesting |
|---|---|---|
| [NESO](https://www.neso.energy/) | Electricity system operation | Demand forecasting, uncertainty, balancing and electricity-system analysis. |
| [National Grid ESO Data Portal](https://data.nationalgrideso.com/) | Energy-system datasets | Historical and operational data suitable for statistical analysis. |
| [Energy Systems Catapult](https://es.catapult.org.uk/) | Energy innovation | Modelling, evidence and analytical approaches to energy-system transformation. |
| [Alan Turing Institute](https://www.turing.ac.uk/news) | Applied data science | Research involving statistical learning, uncertainty and complex systems. |
| [Our World in Data — Energy](https://ourworldindata.org/energy) | Energy statistics | Energy transitions, emissions, electricity generation and global comparisons. |
| [Climate Change Committee](https://www.theccc.org.uk/) | Climate policy | Quantitative assessments, scenarios and uncertainty in climate policy. |

Potential article themes include probabilistic forecasting of electricity demand, renewable generation uncertainty, the interaction between statistical forecasting and mathematical optimisation, and the statistical challenges of evaluating decarbonisation policies.

A particularly attractive theme for the BIS audience would be:

**From prediction to decision: why an accurate statistical forecast is not necessarily the most useful forecast.**

This opens a discussion about decision-dependent loss functions, asymmetric forecasting errors and the value of probabilistic forecasts in optimisation.

## 8. How I would use these resources for an RSS BIS newsletter

Rather than simply collecting links, I would establish a small editorial process that identifies articles with a clear connection to business and industrial statistics.

I would suggest five recurring newsletter sections.

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Statistics in Action** | An interesting real-world application of statistical methodology. |
| **Statistical Thinking** | An article challenging common assumptions about data, uncertainty or inference. |
| **Methods in Focus** | An accessible introduction to a useful statistical technique. |
| **Statistics and Decision-Making** | Examples connecting statistical analysis with operational or strategic decisions. |
| **From the Research Frontier** | Emerging statistical methods with potential industrial or public-sector applications. |

For each selected article, the newsletter could include a short synopsis, the statistical methods involved, the practical relevance and a link to the original source.

## 9. A further idea: build a statistical applications observatory

For the RSS BIS newsletter, I think there is an opportunity to do something more interesting than maintain a conventional reading list.

The Section could establish a continuously updated **Statistical Applications Observatory**, identifying examples of statistics making a difference in business, industry and public administration.

The objective would be to answer three questions:

1. **What statistical problem was addressed?**
2. **What statistical methodology was used?**
3. **What practical difference did it make?**

The observatory could collect articles automatically from selected RSS feeds, blogs and institutional publications. An LLM-assisted pipeline could then classify the articles, extract relevant methodological information, identify duplicates and produce candidate summaries for editorial review.

For example, an article about improving an industrial production process might be classified as:

- **Sector:** Manufacturing
- **Statistical methods:** Design of experiments, regression
- **Application:** Process optimisation
- **Business impact:** Reduced variability and production costs
- **Editorial relevance:** High

Over time, this would produce a searchable collection of real-world statistical applications, potentially useful well beyond the newsletter itself.

**One important editorial principle:** I would avoid focusing exclusively on AI and machine learning. Classical statistical ideas—variation, sampling, uncertainty, experimental design, statistical process control and causal inference—remain fundamental to decision-making in organisations.

A particularly interesting next step would be to identify **20–30 specific articles published during 2025–2026**, rather than just their parent websites, and evaluate them for inclusion in the next RSS BIS newsletter.
